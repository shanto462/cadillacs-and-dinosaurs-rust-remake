# D3e part 2: area walk of Episodes 3, 4 and 5

Design study dated 2026-10-13. Covers internal stages 2, 3 and 4, all six camera areas, their openings, breakables, scenery and placed items. The requested deliverable is this one file, with no game changes or commit. [U: D3e part 2 task brief]

Sources: **R:** repo file or ROM routine/address; **M:** MAME batch and segment; **W:** web document; **U:** user. A source at the end of a paragraph or table row covers that whole paragraph or row. A visual description is an observation, not the official name of a machine. No web claim is needed for the findings below. [R: this file, source convention]

Positions are map `(X, Y, Z)`, in hex unless stated otherwise. Y is height, Z is depth, and larger Z is farther up the screen. A tile controller's position is a script anchor, not a collision box. B numbers are pool B class offsets divided by 4; A and E use the same probe numbering. `G.$xxxx` means RAM `0xFF8000 + xxxx`. [R: capture-recipes.md sections 3 and 4; D3e-pool-b-inventory.md section 1]

## 1. Evidence, method and limits

Read alongside [part 1](D3e-pool-b-inventory.md), [the pre-study](D3e2-prestudy-ep3-5.md), [camera flow](D4c-camera-flow-transitions.md), [items and drops](D4a-items-drops.md), [human enemies and hazards](D3a-humans.md), and the older [Episode 3](../episode-3.md), [Episode 4](../episode-4.md) and [Episode 5](../episode-5.md) notes. The corrections below supersede the older guesses for this scope. [R: named repo files]

Hogg, the playable Cadillac, seating, steering, car damage, radio delivery and Slice's combat are already studied in [D3c-bosses-1.md sections 4 to 6](D3c-bosses-1.md). Use that study for those systems. This file adds their road scenery and prop links. In particular, the car controller is B88; the opening scene car is A16 with A17 wheels. They must not be merged into one class. [R: D3c-bosses-1.md sections 4 to 6; D3a-humans.md section 11]

Exactly four headless MAME processes were used: `d3e2-r1` (58 segments), `d3e2-r2` (30), `d3e2-r3` (70), `d3e2-r4` (75). Inputs and generators are `re/out/d3e2/run1.txt` through `run4.txt` and `gen1.py` through `gen4.py`. Logs, snapshots and contact sheets are under `re/out/probe/d3e2-rN/SEGMENT/`. The wrapper used the dummy SDL drivers and no video or sound device. [R: re/harness/probe/probe.sh; M: d3e2-r1 through d3e2-r4, segments.txt and mame.log]

Run 1 follows the pre-study checklist: three depth sweeps in each area, the opening scenes, and separate checks at its listed camera positions. The sweep walks right for 24 frames, then sends three attack pulses over 30 frames. Runs 2 and 3 test individual props and continue the missed stretches. Run 4 repeats every ordinary container hit with clamp disabled, tests the fence from both sides, and punches a scenery grid every 32 pixels in X and 16 pixels in Z. The scripts use `god on`, watches and kill-all where useful. [R: re/out/d3e2/gen1.py through gen4.py; M: all `sweep-*`, `check-*`, `continue`, `endwalk`, `scenery-grid`, `container-*` and `hit-*` segments]

A sweep is not proof that it reached the end. Some stopped at a barrel, a biker or a wave lock. Targeted hits use `G.$506 = 1` to avoid the screen clamp and item pickup, so they prove the hit and picture, not normal access to that spot. The natural bridge probe in run 3 starts the diagonal descent but does not finish it. Camera jumps into the village can leave dark columns where earlier tile writers were skipped. These are probe artifacts, not missing huts to reproduce. [R: capture-recipes.md section 8; M: d3e2-r1/e5a1-sweep-middle; d3e2-r3/e5-bridge-cross; d3e2-r4/e5-full-bridge-descent]

Contact sheets were inspected for every area, the crash, vent, three B0 breaks, fence break, bridge, fire, buckets, rocks and chief scene. ROM listings and `re/ghidra/decomp.c` were used to separate tile animation from damage and to check routines that the pictures alone cannot identify. Rendered far-layer tile patches are in `re/out/d3e2/far-tiles.png`, generated by `tiles.py`. No ROM table or code is reproduced here. ## 2. Shared interaction contract

Ordinary pails, drums, wooden barrels and tire stacks are E5, with 4 HP. A punch normally breaks them. The hit adds its attack score, then the ordinary break adds 500 points and rolls the container row once. Pails fly off and blink; drums make two pieces; wooden barrels make ten wood puffs; tires make five puffs. Tire stacks cannot be lifted in the measured pickup tests. Pails, drums and wooden barrels can be carried or thrown. [R: 0x28C76, 0x28C8A, D4a-items-drops.md sections 5.1 to 5.6; M: d3e2-r4/*-hit-*; D4a probes cj_* and lift_*]

Explosive containers have `+$5C != 0`, 50 HP, an idle palette shimmer every 51 frames and a fuse after a nonfatal hit. At destruction they make E1 explosion kind 0 and roll their row, with no normal 500-point break award. The explosion can damage fighters and other containers. A water bucket is an ordinary pail, not a fire extinguisher. [R: 0x28E5A, 0x27DD8; D4a-items-drops.md sections 5.7 and 6.3; M: d3e2-r3/e5a0-container-7e8-25c; d3e2-r4/e5a0-hit-7e8-25c]

B0 and B1 use the player-to-prop hit pass, not the container pass. B0 has nominal HP 8, B1 HP 1. A successful normal jab destroyed each tested prop. Their handlers have no item drop call or hostile attack registration. The debris and changed tiles do not become damaging objects. They do not give the E5 container break reward. [R: 0x10CD0, 0x4BFA, 0x74FA2, 0x75238; M: d3e2-r3/e4-break-*; d3e2-r4/e4-fence-dx0-f1]

Tile changes must persist after their B object is freed. Keep a stage-map patch state, or an equivalent background RAM model, so a scroll update does not restore a broken fixture. Respect the 64-column tile ring and separate code, palette, flip and priority bits. A palette update can animate art without changing a tile. [R: D3e-pool-b-inventory.md section 1.4; 0x75046, 0x7507A, 0x75320; R: this file, port requirement derived from those writers]
