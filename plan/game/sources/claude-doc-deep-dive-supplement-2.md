## Operator settings and board hardware
Source for this section is the MAME driver source for the game, read directly from the public MAME repository today: [cps1.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mamedev/mame/master/src/mame/capcom/cps1.cpp) and [cps1_v.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mamedev/mame/master/src/mame/capcom/cps1_v.cpp), plus the [Wikipedia CP System article](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CP_System). Driver data is written by emulator contributors from real boards, so it is more reliable than fan wikis for hardware, though it describes the machine, not the gameplay.
### Settings live in an EEPROM, not DIP switches
In the driver, the original board's two setting banks (DSWA and DSWB) are marked unused because the real settings sit in a 93C46 EEPROM edited through the service and test mode. The only DIP bank in use is DSWC, which holds a single Freeze switch. The service button is a no-toggle input.
The Chinese bootleg Dinosaur Hunter exposes the same settings as ordinary DIP switches, which gives the only readable list of what an operator could change. The bootleg may not match the original's menu exactly, but the values agree with the rank article in the second supplement (eight steps for setting 1 and four for setting 2, defaults in the middle, bonus lives at 500k and 1000k).

### Other regional notes from the driver
The Asia (Taiwan) set, dated 930223, shows a title screen crediting a Taiwanese distributor.

### What this means for a rebuild
The settings table is the list of options to expose in a remake's operator menu, with the defaults above. The ROM sizes bound the amount of art and audio in the original: 4 MB of graphics and 2 MB of samples. The sample budget is useful because it caps how many voice lines and effects the original could hold.
## Soundtrack, audio and the scene structure it reveals
Sources: the full track listing on [Game Music Online](https://www.squareenixmusic.com/albums/t/tenchigsm.shtml) for the 1993 album that carries the game's music (opened), a timed in-game rip on [KHInsider](https://downloads.khinsider.com/game-soundtracks/album/cadillacs-dinosaurs-arcade) (opened), and [VGMdb](https://vgmdb.net/release/12148) and [Discogs](https://www.discogs.com/release/12366080-Capcom-Sound-Team-Tenchi-wo-Kurau-II-The-Battle-of-Red-Cliff-GSM-CAPCOM-7-) for credits (search snippets only). The track names carry stage-and-area codes such as 1-2 and 7-3, which is the strongest evidence yet for how many scenes each stage has.
### Album and credits
The music appears on G.S.M. Capcom 7 (Pony Canyon and Scitron, PCCB-00133, two CDs, 20 August 1993, 3,500 yen), shared with Tenchi wo Kurau II and The Punisher. This game's original tracks are on disc 1 tracks 40–48 and disc 2 tracks 1–22; disc 1 also opens with arranged versions, including one of the swamp-forest theme.
Composers: Isao Abe (credited as Piro2 Oyaji), Syun Nishigaki (Syun) and Kiyo. VGMdb's note identifies Kiyo as Ryo Mizutsuki; that is one site's statement. Sound design: Toshio Kajino (Toshi Bull) and Nobuhiro Oouchi (Nobu).
The CD also holds a 2:40 sound-effects and voice collection for this game, which the rip omits.

### What this adds for a rebuild
Scene count. 22 area slots plus eight boss fights is a concrete target for how many gameplay segments to build.
Music reuse. The original reuses a handful of area tracks across stages (Trap of Silence three times, Forest Poachers twice) and the same boss theme across several bosses, so the music budget is small: roughly 18 area and boss pieces (13 area tracks, 5 boss themes) plus jingles.
Special cases. Boss 4 has a 47-second intro, long enough for the Slice entrance scene, and the final boss has its own intro. The continue screen has two music variants.
Ending. The first-stage theme returns in Ending 2, matching the ending description in the second supplement.

### What this adds for a rebuild
Use the comic's names for dinosaurs in item text, bestiary or credits to stay in-world.
The game's Fessenden is a compression of two sources, a mad scientist and a mutation-obsessed researcher, so its "dinosaur virus" plot is the game's own invention.
Schultz insisted on horizontal, non-tail-dragging dinosaur designs for the cartoon; the game's sprites were made separately, but the guideline is a useful art reference.
## Heroes, HUD and combat numbers from a long FAQ
Source: a republished general FAQ with walkthrough, [Guide Musthopa Dinosaurus Full](http://guidemusthopadinosaurusfull.blogspot.com/) (opened). It is player-written, undated and partly inconsistent with the Korean pages used earlier, so everything here is Unverified; but it records numbers and screen details the other sources omit, such as the select-screen ratings and the HUD layout.
### Select-screen ratings
The character select shows a rating for power, speed and skill plus one named ability. The FAQ lists the on-screen values and says some are inaccurate in practice.
| Hero | Power | Speed | Skill | Ability shown | FAQ author's view | | Jack | 4 | 3 | 3 | Good | Balanced but a little harder to use; best for intermediate players | | Hannah | 2 | 4 | 5 | Item Skilled | Really as strong as Jack and Mustapha; weakness is the shortest reach | | Mustapha | 3 | 5 | 3 | Flying Kicks | Should be power 4 and skill 5; no real weakness, easiest to use | | Mess | 5 | 2 | 4 | High Powered | Slowest, a little more stamina; his running jump attack gets him around fast | The FAQ also explains what the "Item Skilled" label means in play: Hannah's special ability applies to two weapons only, the knife and the broken stick, which she can stab with multiple times where the others stab once.
### HUD and screen layout
Player stats sit at the top of the screen: left to right in order of player number. In a two-player game the health bars are longer than in a three-player game, but hold the same amount.
Each player shows a character icon, a yellow health bar with the character's name, a lives counter after an equals sign, a blue score, and a player rank number between the score and the health bar. The rank is based on score and is not the difficulty rank described in the second supplement.
A second icon below shows the enemy currently being hit, with its name and its own health bar.
A row of bullets with a count appears at the bottom only while a gun is held.
A timer appears mid-screen if the players linger. Running out of time costs a life even at full health, and it affects every player on screen.
Lives per credit vary from 1 to 4 by operator setting. Extra lives come at 300,000 then every 700,000, or at 500,000 then every 1,000,000, or never, by setting.
After a high-score game, initials are entered; if the machine idles, a top-ten screen appears.
### Enemy health structure
| Type | Health | Behavior note | | Standard enemies (grunts, knife men, fat chargers, hunters) | Up to one health bar | Not very aggressive, but dangerous if left standing | | Sub-bosses (Lash, Walther, Bludge, Tyrog 2) | Up to three bars | More aggressive; like bosses with less health | | Dinosaurs | Up to three bars, but the stamina is like a standard enemy | Friendly when first met; angry when attacked | | Zeke | Least of any enemy | A single use of the hero's 360° special defeats it | The FAQ also notes that regular dinosaurs cannot be grabbed, but dinosaur bosses can.
### Combat numbers
The attack combo can be up to four hits and does "major" damage by the FAQ's account; it can end with another hit, a rising attack or a throw. Linking the rising attack is possible during the first three hits but hard after the third.
A grab allows up to three follow-up hits (head punch, knee, knee for most heroes) before a throw. After a three-hit attack combo, up plus attack throws the target forward and down plus attack throws it behind.
Mess's attack combo does about 75% of a boss's health, against about 50% for the others. He needs one fewer hit than others on standard enemies and takes slightly less damage.
The dive kick (attack at the peak of a jump) does roughly 40% to a charging fat enemy when it lands as a counter, and about 20% otherwise. Bosses fall to it only on a counter.
The dash attack can hit several times at range and once up close. Mess's running jump attack is a dash uppercut that knocks down most bosses and can multi-hit.
Running: tap a direction once and then hold it. The run direction can be changed mid-run by rotating the stick through diagonals, and a full circle is possible.
To pick up a barrel press forward and attack beside it. Using the 360° while holding a weapon drops it without health loss if nothing is hit.

This fills the biggest gap in the first document's vehicle section, which had no controls.
The radio sits in the first barrel after two enemies at the bottom. After taking it, a teammate brings the car and the hero jumps in and drives away.
Controls: left slows the car, right speeds it up, and up and down move it. The scroll is automatic.
The driver is invulnerable to everything except Hogg's grenades. A grenade does not hurt the hero's health but damages the car, and the hero dismounts if it takes too many hits. This resolves the earlier contradiction: the car is safe from ordinary enemies but not from the boss.
Rolling barrels appear before the boss; the advice is to slow down then, alternate slow and fast to ram Hogg, and weave up and down to dodge grenades. He throws more grenades as he takes hits.
If the car is lost, the hero jumps out and Hogg runs back and forth at the hero. A punk may drop a shotgun that finishes him quickly.

## Competitive play: speedrun categories and records
Sources: the [Speedrun.com leaderboard](https://www.speedrun.com/cadillacs_and_dinosaurs) for the game (opened), a few run-page and forum snippets, and a YouTube run title (snippet only). The board gives a useful view of how long the game really takes when played well, and which board settings players use.
### Board overview
At the time of reading the board lists 198 runs by 64 players. All runs are on emulated hardware (an arcade emulator or an FPGA recreation); one run page names the FBNeo emulator.
There are 13 categories: Beat The Game; one-credit clears with each of Mess, Mustapha, Jack and Hannah; Co-Op for two players and for three; Deathless one-player runs for each of the four heroes; and Deathless Co-Op for two and for three players.
The Beat The Game board has 27 entries, from 17 minutes 34 seconds to 52 minutes 21 seconds.
Records are set with the default settings. A 24:39 Mustapha run on YouTube states difficulty 1 at level 3 and difficulty 2 at level 1, which matches the defaults reported in the second supplement (3 and 1).
Ranks 3 to 6 sit within a minute and a half of each other; the gap to rank 7 is nearly five minutes.
### What the records suggest
A best-case full clear takes about 17.5 minutes, so the original's content is under 20 minutes when played at maximum efficiency. The slowest ranked clears take 33 to 52 minutes, which is more typical of casual play with continues and cutscenes.
Two-player Co-Op leaders were at about 25 minutes 20 seconds in the recent-runs list at the time of reading.
A forum thread on the board asks for a separate "full damage" category. The posters say the state depends on randomness and on taking some hits deliberately, and that staying in it means avoiding damage afterward. This looks like the low-health damage boost described in the second supplement, but the thread does not say so, so treat the link as an inference.
One-credit clears are tracked separately for each of the four heroes, so all four are treated as viable for a full clear.
